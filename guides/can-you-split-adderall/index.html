Can you split Adderall?

Adderall IR tablets can be split when your prescriber orders a half dose. Most extended-release ADHD medications can't. Here's what each label says, how to split cleanly, and how to keep your count straight.

By the Halftab team · Checked against FDA prescribing information · Updated September 25, 2026

The short answer

  • Adderall IR tablets are scored, and prescribers sometimes order half-tab doses. Split one only if your prescriber has told you to.
  • Adderall XR capsules should never be divided. You can open one and sprinkle all of it on applesauce, but not take part of it.
  • Never crush, chew, or split extended-release medications like Concerta. It can release the whole day's dose at once.

Which ADHD medications can be split?

It comes down to how each one is built. Immediate-release tablets are usually the same all the way through. Extended-release products rely on a coating, beads, or a pump shell to spread the dose out, and cutting them breaks that.

Why people split Adderall

  • A prescribed half dose. The dose you need falls between two strengths, or your prescriber is starting low.
  • A smaller afternoon dose. Some schedules use a full tab in the morning and a half tab later, so less is still active at bedtime.
  • Tapering. Stepping down gradually under your prescriber's direction.
Splitting to take more or less than prescribed, or to stretch a supply, changes your dose. Talk to your prescriber first, especially during shortages. They may be able to switch strengths or formulations instead.

How to split a tablet cleanly

  1. Use a pill splitter, not a knife or your fingers. They cost a few dollars at any pharmacy and give much more even halves.
  2. Line the blade up with the score, the groove across the tablet, and press straight down.
  3. Split close to when you'll take it. Keep any spare half in the original bottle, away from moisture.
  4. If a tablet crumbles, don't guess at the dose. Ask your pharmacist what to do.

Keeping count when you split

Halves make the math harder. Two half tabs of 20 mg Adderall are 20 mg, not 40. A bottle of 60 tablets lasts longer if some doses are halves. It's easy to lose track of your daily total and of when you'll need a refill. Two habits help:

  • Log in tablets and milligrams. Write down “½ tab, 10 mg” instead of just “Adderall” so your daily total stays right.
  • Count your supply in halves. If you split regularly, what's in the bottle changes by half a tablet at a time. Count it that way so your refill request isn't too early or too late.

Does a half tab last as long as a whole one?

Roughly. A half tab of Adderall IR follows the same basic timeline, starting in 30 to 60 minutes and fading after about 4 hours, but at a lower level. Because it's a smaller effect to begin with, it can drop below the point where you notice it a little sooner. See how long Adderall lasts for the full timeline.

Common questions

Can you cut Adderall in half?

Adderall IR tablets are scored and can be split in half when a prescriber orders a half-tab dose. Use a pill splitter and cut along the score. Adderall XR capsules should never be divided.

Can you split Adderall XR?

No. Adderall XR capsules release half the dose right away and half about four hours later. You can open a capsule and sprinkle the entire contents on applesauce, but you shouldn't take part of a capsule or chew the beads.

Can you crush Adderall?

Don't crush Adderall unless your prescriber or pharmacist tells you to. Crushing extended-release forms can release the whole dose at once, and the prescribing information warns about misuse.

Is it safe to split Concerta?

No. Concerta must be swallowed whole. It uses a timed-release shell that doesn't work if it's cut, chewed, or crushed.

Does splitting a pill change how it works?

For a scored immediate-release tablet like Adderall IR, a half tab works the same way at a lower dose. For extended-release products, splitting can break the release system and change how the dose is delivered, which is why most of them must be swallowed whole.
